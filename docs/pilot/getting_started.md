# Getting started with Argus — pilot guide

Welcome. This is the one-page guide for your pilot. It tells you what Argus does,
how to get set up, what you get per engagement, how your team collaborates, and —
just as importantly — what Argus **can't** do yet, so you know exactly where to
keep a human in the loop.

---

## What Argus does (the honest version)

Argus turns a partner's engagement brief into a complete, **evidence-backed**
consulting deliverable set — drafted, verified, and ready for your review.

The wedge is **verification**, not generation. Plenty of tools will write a
confident-sounding memo. Argus's job is to make sure every claim in that memo is
actually supported by the evidence behind it — your uploaded library plus the
research it runs — and to flag the claims that aren't. You get the leverage of an
AI analyst with a verification layer that's calibrated against your own standard,
not a vendor's marketing.

**Where the verifier stands today (measured, not claimed):** on a batch of 61
real engagement claims we labelled by hand and scored through the cross-family
verifier, **every claim it marked "supported" was actually supported** (zero
false positives), and it **caught 100% of the genuinely unsupported claims**. The
trade-off is that it errs on the side of caution: it down-graded many genuinely-
supported claims to "needs review" rather than risk a wrong "supported." In
practice that means **you'll see more claims flagged for a human look than is
strictly necessary — but you should almost never see a wrong claim wearing a
"verified" badge.** That's the deliberate direction: under-credit, never
over-claim.

## The 4 onboarding steps

You can do these in the app (the onboarding wizard) or your Argus contact can set
them up with you offline. Either way, the steps are the same:

1. **Firm setup** — your firm name and branding (primary colour, footer text,
   optional logo). Branding flows through to every deliverable so client-facing
   documents look like yours, not ours.
2. **Invite your team** — add a partner and 1–3 consultants/analysts, each with a
   role: **firm admin** (can manage the firm, library, and team) or **firm
   member** (works on engagements).
3. **Upload your library** — drag in your own playbooks, sector primers,
   methodologies, comparable-transaction sheets, and prior reports. Argus ingests
   them so your engagements draw on **your** firm's knowledge. We do **not** seed
   any synthetic or sample content into your firm — your library is yours alone.
   Supported file types: PDF, Word (.docx), Markdown, and plain text.
4. **Create your first engagement** — write a brief, pick a mode (M&A diligence,
   growth strategy, or general decision support), and assign a lead and a
   reviewer. There are example briefs in your library for reference — they show
   what a good, evidence-rich brief looks like. Use them as a model, not a
   template to copy.

## What you get per engagement — the 6 deliverables

Every engagement produces a consistent deliverable set, all built from the same
verified analysis:

1. **Memo** — the full written analysis: recommendation, reasoning, evidence,
   risks, and counterarguments.
2. **One-pager** — the executive summary on a single page.
3. **Slide deck** — a client-ready PowerPoint.
4. **Excel model** — the financial model (proforma, comparables, sensitivities)
   where the mode calls for one.
5. **Client email** — a cover note you can send with the deliverables.
6. **Interview guide** — the diligence questions worth asking next, derived from
   the gaps the analysis couldn't close from the evidence on hand.

Every claim in these carries its verification status, so you and your client can
see what's solid and what needs a second look.

## How your team collaborates (review → comments → approval, in 60 seconds)

1. A consultant runs an engagement and the deliverables draft.
2. They **submit for review** — the assigned reviewer (usually a partner) gets
   notified.
3. The reviewer reads the deliverables, leaves **comments** anchored to specific
   sections or claims, and either **requests changes** or **approves**.
4. On approval, the engagement is locked and ready to deliver to the client.

Comments, mentions, and review state are all tracked, so nothing falls through
the cracks and there's a clear audit trail of who approved what.

## Honest limitations — where to keep a human in the loop

We'd rather tell you this up front than have you discover it in front of a client.

- **The verifier is conservative, not omniscient.** It's excellent at not
  blessing a wrong claim, but it will flag plenty of *correct* claims as "needs
  review." Treat a flag as "a human should glance at this," not "this is wrong."
- **What it can't reliably catch.** Subtle misattribution (a real fact credited
  to the wrong source), claims that are true in general but wrong for *this*
  specific context, and reasoning errors that aren't about a single factual claim.
  These are the cases where your reviewer's judgement is irreplaceable.
- **It's only as good as your evidence.** Argus verifies claims against your
  library and the research it runs. If the evidence to confirm a claim simply
  isn't there, Argus will say "I can't support this yet" and tell you what
  question would close the gap — it won't invent support.
- **This is a pilot.** The verification posture above is **"AI-assisted with
  human review on flagged claims."** That is the intended, honest posture for the
  pilot — not a limitation to apologise for. A partner reviews before anything
  reaches a client. Don't ship an Argus deliverable to a client without that
  human review step.
