Our client is a diversified industrial-services group reviewing an
underperforming business line that has missed plan for six consecutive
quarters. The board needs a recommendation: **turn it around, divest it, or
wind it down** — with a decision at the next quarterly board meeting.

The engagement library has: the line's three-year P&L and cash flows, a list of
its top customers (one of which is a strategic relationship for the wider
group), and headcount by function. The strategic-customer relationship and the
employee impact are real constraints, not footnotes.

The recommendation should cover:

1. **The honest financial picture.** Contribution margin and cash burn of the
   line on a standalone basis, and the point at which continued investment
   stops being rational on the numbers alone.

2. **Turnaround case.** Are there one or two levers that could credibly return
   the line to break-even within a defined window, and what would they cost and
   risk? Be skeptical — six missed quarters is a strong prior.

3. **Divestiture option.** Is there a plausible buyer pool, and a rough value
   range from comparables? What's the realistic timeline and deal risk?

4. **Recommendation.** A clear call across turn-around / divest / wind-down,
   weighing the strategic-customer and employee impacts explicitly against the
   financials — and the downside scenario the board should plan for under the
   recommended path.

Where the buyer pool or turnaround upside can't be evidenced, say so; the board
needs the uncertainty named, not smoothed over.
