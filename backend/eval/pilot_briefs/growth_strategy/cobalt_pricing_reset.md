Our client sells a subscription analytics product (~£40m revenue, mostly mid-
market) whose pricing has drifted — discounting is ad hoc and the packaging
hasn't changed in three years. The CEO wants a recommendation: **how should we
restructure pricing and packaging to lift ARPA and net revenue retention
without triggering churn?**

The engagement library has: 18 months of deal-level pricing data (list vs
realised price, discount depth by segment and rep), the current three-tier
packaging, and a churned-customer exit-survey summary. The exit survey is the
honest signal on what customers actually value — weight it.

The recommendation needs to cover:

1. **Where value and price have decoupled.** Using the deal data, identify
   which segments are systematically under- or over-priced relative to the
   value they get, and what's driving the discount leakage.

2. **Value metric.** Assess whether the current per-seat metric still aligns
   with how customers get value, or whether a usage/outcome metric would
   capture more of it. Compare against how the two or three nearest competitors
   package.

3. **Elasticity and churn risk.** Estimate the realistic churn exposure of a
   re-pricing on the most-affected segments, and design the change to cap that
   downside (grandfathering, phased migration).

4. **Recommendation.** A revised packaging and pricing structure, the expected
   ARPA/NRR impact with a range, and a migration plan that protects the
   at-risk cohorts.

Where elasticity can't be estimated from the data on hand, say what test
(a pricing pilot on one segment) would close the gap before a full rollout.
