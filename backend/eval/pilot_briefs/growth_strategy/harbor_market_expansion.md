mode: growth_strategy

    "how should we grow", supplies the constraint that decides it (team cost and
    data-residency rules), and asks for a recommendation with the one assumption

research_targets: market size and growth by geography, competitive intensity,
    regulatory and data-residency cost, go-to-market entry cost, Porter's five forces

Our client is a B2B SaaS scale-up (~£25m ARR, UK + Benelux today) deciding
where to expand next. They've narrowed it to **France or Germany** and need a
recommendation for the board: **which market first, and what's the entry model
and 18-month plan?**

In the engagement library: the client's current new-logo economics (CAC,
sales-cycle length, ACV by segment) and a competitor landscape doc their
product team maintains. Anchor the entry-cost analysis on the client's actual
CAC, not market averages.

What the recommendation turns on:

1. **Market attractiveness.** Size the addressable B2B SaaS spend in each
   market and its growth, and assess competitive intensity (use Porter's five
   forces — the board expects that lens). Where is the client's wedge least
   contested?

2. **Cost and friction of entry.** Quantify the realistic cost to stand up a
   go-to-market motion in each — local team, data-residency/compliance
   infrastructure (Germany's requirements are heavier), and the sales-cycle
   drag of a new market. Net this against the client's existing unit economics.

3. **Entry model.** For the recommended market, compare a direct-sales build
   vs a partner-led motion for the first 18 months, with the trade-off stated
   plainly.

4. **Recommendation.** A clear first-market pick, an entry model, a phased
   18-month plan, and explicitly: the single assumption that, if wrong, would
   flip the recommendation to the other market.

Be honest where the public data is thin — a ranged estimate with the source
named beats a precise-looking number with no provenance.
