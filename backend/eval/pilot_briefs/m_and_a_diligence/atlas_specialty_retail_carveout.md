Our client (a mid-market PE fund, £400m fund III) is considering a bid for the
Home & Living division being carved out of a listed UK specialty retailer. The
decision we need to support by the next investment committee (three weeks out):
**should the fund proceed to a binding offer, and if so, at what EV range?**

What we already have, attached to the engagement library: the vendor CIM, three
years of divisional P&L (FY22–FY24), and the draft TSA term sheet. Treat the
CIM's adjusted-EBITDA bridge as a vendor claim to be challenged, not a fact.

The questions that decide this:

1. **Standalone economics.** The division shares distribution, IT, and head-
   office functions with the parent. What is the realistic standalone EBITDA
   once stranded costs and TSA exit costs are loaded in? The vendor shows a
   12.4% margin; we suspect 9–10% standalone is closer to the truth and want
   that triangulated against comparable carve-outs.

2. **Revenue quality.** Like-for-like growth has been positive but the mix
   shifted toward promotional volume. Decompose LFL into traffic, basket, and
   price/mix, and flag whether the FY24 growth is durable or pulled-forward.

3. **Integration risk.** The fund has no platform in this sector, so this is a
   standalone hold. What are the top operational risks in running the division
   off the parent's systems once the TSA lapses (target 12 months)?

4. **Valuation.** Triangulate an EV range from comparable transactions and a
   simple standalone DCF. We want a recommendation with a defensible range, the
   key value drivers, and the two or three assumptions the IC should stress.

Be explicit where the evidence is thin — a "we can't yet support X without
management Q&A on Y" is more useful to the IC than a confident guess.
