Our client runs a healthcare-services buy-and-build (private outpatient
clinics, ~14 sites across the platform today). They've found a regional operator
of 5 clinics they'd acquire as the next platform asset. The decision for the
operating partner: **does this asset fit the roll-up thesis at a price that
preserves the platform's blended multiple on exit?**

The engagement library holds: the target's management accounts, the platform's
own unit economics (mature-clinic margin curve, ramp profile for newly-opened
sites), and a one-page summary of the platform's reimbursement mix. The
client's whole thesis rests on margin convergence — newly-acquired clinics
reaching platform-level margins within 18 months — so test that, don't assume it.

The decisive questions:

1. **Reimbursement and regulatory exposure.** What share of the target's
   revenue depends on public vs private-pay, and what's the exposure to a
   tariff or commissioning change over the hold? This is the risk that can
   break the thesis.

2. **Growth quality.** Split the target's recent growth into organic
   (same-site) vs acquired/new-site, and assess whether the same-site trend
   supports the platform's margin-convergence assumption.

3. **Margin bridge to platform.** Build the bridge from the target's current
   site-level margin to the platform's mature-clinic margin, and identify the
   two or three levers (staffing model, procurement, payer mix) that actually
   close the gap — and how long each realistically takes.

4. **Valuation.** Triangulate an EV range from the platform's prior acquisition
   multiples and a site-level DCF, and state the price above which the deal
   dilutes the platform's blended exit multiple.

Flag any claim that needs site visits or payer-contract review to confirm — the
operating partner would rather know the gap than get a clean-looking answer.
