Our client is a strategic acquirer (PE-backed B2B software platform, ~£60m ARR)
evaluating a bolt-on: a vertical SaaS provider serving UK and Irish logistics
operators. The board decision we're supporting: **approve a non-binding offer
and the synergy case that justifies the premium?**

In the engagement library: the target's data-room financials (ARR waterfall,
cohort retention, top-20 customer list with contract end dates) and our client's
own integration playbook from two prior bolt-ons. Use the playbook — the client
wants the diligence to reflect how they actually integrate, not a textbook view.

What decides the recommendation:

1. **Revenue durability.** Net revenue retention is reported at 108%. Validate
   that against the cohort data and flag whether it's carried by a small number
   of expanding accounts. Quantify customer concentration — what share of ARR
   sits in the top 5 logos, and when do those contracts renew?

2. **Synergy case.** The deal model assumes £3.2m of run-rate synergies
   (£1.9m cost, £1.3m cross-sell revenue). Break this down and rank each line
   by confidence — which synergies are contracted/structural vs aspirational?
   We will not present a single summed number to the board without that ranking.

3. **Churn risk on change of control.** Logistics customers are sticky but
   integration-sensitive. What's the realistic churn exposure in the 12 months
   post-close, and which contract terms (change-of-control clauses, auto-renew)
   matter?

4. **Integration risk and cost.** Using the client's playbook, identify the
   top integration risks and a rough one-time integration cost the board should
   net against the synergy case.

Where a synergy or retention claim can't be supported from the data room, say
so and name the management question that would close the gap.
